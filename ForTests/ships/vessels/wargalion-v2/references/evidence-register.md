|---|---|---|---|
| Historical reference | San Martín / São Martinho, Portuguese-built galleon later in Spanish service | Naval historical publications and secondary accounts | Reference label only; this is not an exact replica |
| Working length and beam | 39.0 × 11.7 m in *Revista General de Marina* (2007) | Model report, not an original shipyard dimension schedule | Retained working scene scale |

| Other published estimate | 27.0 m keel length, 11.15 m beam and 4.0 m to first deck, attributed to Augusto Salgado in the same 2012 publication | Different estimate and measurement basis | Preserved as a separate estimate; not averaged with Casado Soto |
| Hull profile | Reproduced San Martín plan sheet | Secondary reproduction, not a calibrated original offsets table | Qualitative side and section guide; station values remain reconstruction estimates |
| Waterline / topsides | Spanish naval description says the hull was widest at the waterline and narrowed toward the bulwarks | General description of galleon form, not a measured San Martín offsets table | Maximum section breadth aligned with estimated waterline; upper hull tapers inward |
| Hull shape context | Portuguese 16th-century rising-and-narrowing research | Peer-reviewed general method research | Context only; not proof of this vessel's exact design process |
| Comparative plans | Pinterest and generic Elizabethan galleon drawings | Comparative imagery, not San Martín-specific | Broad visual cross-check only |
| Aftcastle | Reproduced model plan and comparative historical galleon imagery | Comparative reconstruction evidence | Three stepped deck levels and galleries remain a visual hypothesis |
| Exact rig | No calibrated rig drawing has been transcribed for this build | Unresolved | Deferred from this stage |

## Required before historical calibration

1. Obtain a high-resolution plan with traceable scale marks.
2. Record pixel-to-metre calibration and uncertainty for every digitized station.
3. Cross-check body-plan sections against waterline and buttock families from the common surface.
4. Compare stern and forecastle profiles against attributable period evidence.
5. Revisit the principal dimensions if stronger primary measurements become available.
